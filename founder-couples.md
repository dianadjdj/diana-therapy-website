Two Kinds of Couple

## The couple who run it together, and the couple who live with it.

This page covers two relationships that get treated as different problems and behave like the same one.

### Married and running the company together

Both of you are founders, or one of you founded it and the other now runs half of it. The business conversation is sharp and constant. The marriage conversation has gone quiet underneath it. You sit in the same board meeting and come home to the same kitchen, and there is no room in the house where the company is not.

### One partner is the founder, the other is not

One of you runs a company. The other has a career, or the children, or both, and a front-row seat to what the company takes. The strain is a company that takes up more of the house than anyone agreed to, and a partner who cannot say so without sounding like they are against the thing.

This page is not for a co-founder pair with no romantic relationship. That is a different piece of work, and a founder in that situation can bring it to individual sessions through the [founder hub](therapy-for-founders.html).

I am Gottman-trained and work with both kinds of couple. Sessions are 50 minutes, $530, by secure video, for people in California and Florida. The three reads below are where to start.

How the Work Goes

## Two shapes of the same problem.

### When you are also co-founders

Describe a strained co-founder relationship to a couples therapist without mentioning the company and they will recognize all of it: reading a message twice, once for content and once for tone, the dread before the Monday one-on-one, the scorekeeping. When the co-founders are married, there is no leaving it at the office. Founders tend to know the Wasserman number, that about 65 percent of high-potential startups fail on conflict inside the founding team, and still treat the relationship as a management problem. Operating principles in a shared drive. A shared calendar to fix a marriage.

What is missing matters more than what is present. Couples who run a company together become very good at the business conversation, clear and efficient and low-drama, and let the other conversation atrophy. Nobody has asked "do you still trust me" or said "I did not feel respected in that meeting" in over a year, and both people can feel the missing conversation without being able to start it. The scripts from the books on difficult conversations hold for about eleven minutes, until one person's voice goes a half step flatter than it needs to and the other hears the last two years inside it. That is a regulation problem, and it comes before the communication problem.

### When one of you is the founder

This couple arrives with a different shape. The founder is often the one who books, sometimes for something else, and gets to the marriage within the first month. The partner has been carrying the house, and the children if there are any, through a fundraise or a layoff or a pivot they did not choose and could not vote on. What they say in the first session is some version of: I am proud of you, and I cannot keep doing this. The founder hears an ultimatum. It is usually a request to be counted.

The company is in the room whether or not it is on the agenda. Part of the work is naming what it has cost, out loud, with both people present, and deciding together what it gets to take from here. Part of it is the founder learning to come home without the board meeting. Part of it is the partner getting to say what they want without it landing as an attack on the company.

In both cases the work starts lower down than most couples therapy: with what each person's nervous system is doing in the room, and with the trust that dropped without anyone naming it. Often it starts with one of you alone. The other comes later, or does not, and the work still holds.

Common Questions

## Asked before booking.

We are married and co-founders. Is this therapy or mediation?

Therapy. Mediation settles a dispute: equity, titles, who owns which decision. Therapy works on the relationship that keeps producing the disputes. Co-founder conflicts get argued like strategy disagreements and behave like marriage problems, and when the co-founders are married they are marriage problems. A mediated agreement does not touch the tone-checking, the scorekeeping, or the trust that quietly dropped a year ago. If you need a term sheet resolved, a mediator is the right call. If the same fight keeps coming back in new clothes, that is the work here.

Do both of us need to be in California or Florida?

Yes. I am licensed in California (LMFT #105546) and Florida (TPMF785), and each person needs to be physically in one of those states at the time of the session. You do not need to be in the same state as each other, and you do not need to be in the same room. A couple on separate video connections, one in San Francisco and one in Miami, is a normal setup.

What if my partner does not want therapy?

Then you come alone. A lot of the couples work in my practice starts that way: one person books for something else and gets to the marriage within the first month. Working on your side of a two-person pattern changes the pattern. Sometimes the other person joins later. Sometimes they do not, and the work is still worth doing.

Do you see founders whose partner is not in the industry?

Yes, and often. The founder with a partner who is not in the industry is one of the two kinds of couple this page is for. The strain there is rarely about the company's decisions. It is about who gets to say what the company has cost the house, and whether saying it counts as being against the thing.

Do you work with co-founders who are not a couple?

Not as couples therapy. This page is for romantic couples, married or partnered: the two of you running a company together, or one of you the founder and the other not. A co-founder pair with no romantic relationship is a different piece of work. If you are a founder in a strained co-founder relationship, bring it to individual sessions. That is a normal part of founder work and the [founder hub](therapy-for-founders.html) covers it.
