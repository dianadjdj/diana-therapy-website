Two Kinds of Couple

The marriage and the partnership.

This page covers two relationships that get treated as different problems and behave like the same one. The first is the romantic couple where one or both people run a company: the founder and the spouse who is not in the industry, the dual-career household where one person just closed a round and the other is stuck, the co-founder couple who are also married. The second is the co-founder pair with no romance in it at all, bound legally and financially, spending more waking hours together than with anyone else, and no longer able to have the conversation that matters.

I am Gottman-trained and work with both. Sessions are 50 minutes, $530, by secure video, for people in California and Florida. The three reads below are where to start.

Co-founders

How co-founder work differs.

Describe a strained co-founder relationship to a couples therapist without mentioning the company and they will recognize all of it: reading a message twice, once for content and once for tone, the dread before the Monday one-on-one, the scorekeeping. Structurally it is a marriage with worse exit options. Founders tend to know the Wasserman number, that about 65 percent of high-potential startups fail on conflict inside the founding team, and still treat the relationship as a management problem. Operating principles in a shared drive. A shared calendar to fix a marriage.

The difference from romantic couples work is what is missing rather than what is present. Co-founders have usually become very good at the business conversation, clear and efficient and low-drama, and have let the other conversation atrophy. Nobody has asked "do you still trust me" or said "I felt expendable in that meeting" in over a year, and both people can feel the missing conversation without being able to start it. The scripts from the books on difficult conversations hold for about eleven minutes, until one person's voice goes a half step flatter than it needs to and the other hears the last two years inside it. That is a regulation problem, and it comes before the communication problem.

So the work with co-founders starts lower down than it does with most romantic couples: with what each person's nervous system is doing in the room, and with the trust that dropped without anyone naming it. Often it starts with one founder alone. The pair comes later, or does not, and the work still holds.
